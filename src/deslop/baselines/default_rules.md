# Rules

These rules are enforced by the converter. Calibration appends voice-derived
rules below the default set. Edit freely; the converter reads whatever is here.

## Default

- Keep every citation, quotation, number, and proper name exactly as given.
- Do not make the text the agent of an intention. Chapters do not ask, insist,
  or refuse; writers and characters do.
- Do not end a paragraph on an abstract aphorism. End on a particular: a
  quotation, a name, a fact, or a plain claim about the matter at hand.
- Do not frame a point as "not X but Y" or "X, not Y" when a direct statement
  will do.
- No balance language: "at once", "tension", "hinge", "complicates",
  "holds together", "at the same time".
- Do not stack three parallel items for rhythm. Use three items only when
  there are three things.
- Do not coin possessive abstractions ("appetite's arithmetic").
- Do not narrate what the text or the essay is doing. State the point.
- Do not pose a question you do not answer in the next sentence.
- No stock openers: "So", "Then,", "Against that,", "Set X beside Y",
  "It is worth noting", "Importantly".
- One hedge per sentence at most. Prefer none.
- No sincerity intensifiers: deeply, truly, fundamentally, inherently,
  genuinely, profoundly.
- Do not use a colon to reveal a single abstract noun.
- Do not close with a paragraph that restates the earlier paragraphs.
- Do not replace a name with an epithet ("the Danish prince") once the name
  has been given.
